1. What HerCare is, and what it is not

  • A free period tracker and wellness helper: users record period dates, symptoms and mood, get reminders, and see general self-care tips.
  • It does NOT diagnose, treat or advise on any condition. It does not infer pregnancy, PCOS or any other diagnosis from dates.
  • It is NOT a contraception or fertility tool. It never shows ovulation, fertile days or pregnancy information.
  • Every prediction is shown as an estimate, never as a fact.
  • Hydration reminders never state an amount of water. Activity tips are always optional, and the user can choose to rest instead.
  • While a period is logged, a few extra general tips are shown. That is a simple rule on the user's own dates.

4. How the next-period estimate works

  • The app looks at the number of days between the start dates the user has recorded. Gaps shorter than 15 days or longer than 60 days are ignored (for example when a period was forgotten).
  • With at least 2 usable gaps, the next period is estimated from the average of the most recent 6. With fewer, the app uses the cycle length the user chose (default 28 days) and says there is too little history.
  • The usual period length is the average of recently recorded lengths, otherwise the user's choice (default 5 days).
  • The calendar shows the next 3 estimated periods, clearly marked as estimates and different from logged days.
  • If the estimated date passes without a logged period, the app says it is "late by N days" and offers no explanation.
  • The app never calls a cycle "irregular", "normal" or "abnormal", and never suggests a cause.

6. Questions for you

  1. Are the symptom, flow, mood and energy options appropriate and easy to understand? Is anything missing, or anything that should not be offered?
  2. Should the app show a short "when to seek care" message, for example for very heavy bleeding or severe pain? A draft to edit: "If you have very heavy bleeding, severe pain, or anything that worries you, please speak to a doctor or nurse."
  3. Are the estimate settings acceptable (cycles outside 15 to 60 days ignored when averaging; defaults of 28 and 5 days)? Should the app suggest seeing a doctor after a very long gap or very irregular cycles? Today it deliberately does not.
  4. What minimum age, or wording about who the app is for, should the welcome screen state?
  5. Are the food tips suitable for a broad audience? Should the wording mention dietary needs, allergies, vegetarian or vegan users, or conditions where the iron tip may not suit?
  6. Are the activity tips, and offering "rest today" instead, appropriate? Is "stop if pain or discomfort gets worse" enough?
  7. Are hydration reminders without any amount of water acceptable?
  8. Are the disclaimer and privacy wording accurate and understandable? Is there anything to add?
  9. Is there anything users should never be told, or must always be told?
